Direction of effect

← Index

Which methylation changes are consistent with a gene being turned down or up.

In plain terms

Now we translate 'methylation went up or down' into 'the gene was probably turned down or up.' The rule we trust: in a promoter, more methylation tends to switch a gene off (silencing) and less tends to switch it on (activation). Outside promoters that rule is unreliable, so we deliberately refuse to guess and mark those spots 'ambiguous.' The bar shows how all the significant changes split across those three buckets.

Direction of effect is read conservatively, and only inside promoters, where the relationship is best established: a methylated promoter is associated with a gene being expressed at a lower level; an unmethylated one with higher expression. Outside promoters the relationship can reverse, so those sites are left unlabelled.

A deliberate limit
These are associations predicted from methylation, not measurements of gene expression. Labelling only promoter sites, and marking everything else as ambiguous, keeps the interpretation within what the data support.
Key takeaway

We can point to roughly 99,000 likely gene-silencing and 61,000 likely gene-activating events, while honestly leaving the majority (outside promoters) unlabelled rather than overclaiming.
