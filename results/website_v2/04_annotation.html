Placing each site in its exact genomic position and regulatory context.

In plain terms

A changed spot only means something once you know where it sits. Here we label every spot with its exact position and, crucially, whether it falls in a gene's on-switch region (the promoter), inside the gene body, or between genes. The bar chart shows how the spots are distributed across those regions. This step is pure book-keeping, but it is what makes the next step's interpretation trustworthy — the same amount of methylation means different things in a promoter versus a gene body.

Every array site was placed in its genomic context: its exact hg38 coordinate, the gene it belongs to, whether it falls in a promoter, gene body or between genes, and its CpG-island relationship. This context is what allows a later, cautious reading of direction: promoter methylation and gene-body methylation are not equivalent.

Key takeaway
