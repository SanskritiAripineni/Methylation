Where tumor and normal tissue differ in methylation, across roughly 485,000 sites.

In plain terms

This is the core comparison. For each of the ~485,000 spots we take the average methylation in tumours and in normals and measure the difference (called Δβ), then test whether that difference is bigger than we would expect by chance — while accounting for the fact that we are running 485,000 tests at once. The volcano plot puts every spot on one picture: how big the change is (left–right) against how sure we are it is real (up). It forms two 'wings' because tumours both add methylation at some spots and remove it at others.

Effect size for methylation is Δβ, a difference in methylation fraction — not log-fold-change (which applies to expression data). Significance is a per-site Welch's t-test with Benjamini–Hochberg FDR across all sites. Read the shape and spread rather than absolute height, which scales with sample size.

Key takeaway

Tens of thousands of spots change reliably between tumour and normal, in both directions — breast tumours carry a large, systematic methylation difference, not random noise.
