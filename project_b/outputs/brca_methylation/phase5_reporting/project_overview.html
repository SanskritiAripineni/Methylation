Project B Overview

Breast Cancer Methylation Project

This project used public breast cancer methylation data to find DNA sites that look different in tumor tissue compared with normal tissue, build a smaller biomarker panel, test that panel on another dataset, and turn the results into a simple research-style reporting system.

What We Did

Phase 1

Build the baseline pipeline

We loaded TCGA breast cancer methylation data, matched the sample IDs to the clinical information, checked data quality, filtered the probes, and compared tumor tissue with normal tissue.

  • 791 tumor samples and 97 normal samples were kept.
  • 382,924 probes were kept after filtering.
  • The result was a large list of CpG sites that changed strongly between tumor and normal tissue.
Phase 2

Add subtype comparisons

We expanded the pipeline so it could also compare specific tumor groups instead of only tumor vs normal.

  • We used Basal as a practical TNBC-like subtype proxy.
  • We ran Basal vs non-Basal and subtype-vs-normal comparisons.
  • This helped separate general tumor markers from subtype-skewed markers.
Phase 3

Build a smaller biomarker panel

We reduced the long CpG lists into a cleaner panel that is easier to use and explain.

  • 12 CpGs were chosen as general tumor markers.
  • 8 CpGs were chosen as Basal-skewed markers.
  • The final refined panel contains 20 CpGs total.
Phase 4

Validate the panel

We tested whether the smaller panel still worked well, both inside TCGA and on a separate public dataset.

  • Internal tumor-vs-normal ROC AUC was 0.996.
  • External validation on GSE66695 gave ROC AUC 0.9997.
  • The signal stayed strong across Stage I, II, III, and IV tumors.
Phase 5

Build a simple report layer

We turned the panel into a clean reporting system with score bands and simple example reports.

  • Low score means the sample looks more like normal tissue.
  • High score means the sample looks more like tumor tissue.
  • We kept the language research-safe and avoided overclaiming.

Main Results

Simple summary: The methylation panel separated normal breast tissue from breast tumor tissue extremely well, and it also captured a useful Basal-skewed subtype signal.

  • The best internal tumor-vs-normal performance was ROC AUC 0.996.
  • The external validation dataset also worked very well, with ROC AUC 0.9997.
  • The final panel is small enough to explain clearly and strong enough to keep testing in later work.

How to Read the Final Score

Low

A score at or below 0.447 usually falls in the range seen in normal-like samples.

Indeterminate

A score between 0.447 and 0.835 sits in the overlap zone and should be treated carefully.

High

A score at or above 0.835 usually falls in the range seen in tumor-like samples.

This is still a research-use result, not a clinical diagnosis. The score should be interpreted alongside pathology, imaging, and other medical information.

Glossary

DNA methylation
A chemical tag on DNA that can help turn genes on or off.
CpG site
A specific place in DNA where methylation is measured.
Probe
A measurement spot on the methylation array that reads one CpG site.
Beta value
A number between 0 and 1 that shows how methylated a CpG site is.
Delta beta
The difference in methylation between two groups, such as tumor and normal.
FDR
False discovery rate. It helps control for false positives when testing many CpG sites at once.
Biomarker panel
A smaller set of markers chosen because they are especially useful and informative.
Subtype
A smaller biological subgroup inside a cancer type. In this project, Basal is one important breast cancer subtype.
ROC AUC
A score that tells how well a model separates two groups. Closer to 1 means better separation.
Validation
Testing whether the model still works on new data instead of only the data used to build it.
